## Areas to look into
- **Rabia / west Nineveh (around 36.75–36.78 N, 42.35–42.40 E): possible cereal undercount.**
  - Field 3 and its two big neighbours (145 ha and 181 ha) were all green on 8–13 May 2026 and turned straw-coloured together by about 25 May. That points to one crop across the area, most likely winter cereal.
  - The map labels the neighbours about 47% cereal on WorldCereal's top-crop map, but only 4–11% confident cereal. The rest is "vegetables" (~48%), which is implausible on a large rain-fed plain.
  - The 70% rule then hides most of the cereal there.
  - Field 4 turned out NOT to be an undercount: its odd labels marked a genuinely different, spring-sown strip.
  - Field 11 (clearly cereal) is mapped 100% wheat by WorldCereal's top crop, but 37% of it falls below the 70% rule as "wheat doubtful". So even where the field verdict is right, the area total in this belt is undercounted.
  - Field 16 (Shekhan–Akre, random draw) shows the same effect outside the Rabia belt: clearly cereal, 92% cereal on the top-crop map, but 46% of it "wheat doubtful", leaving the field verdict at 51%, just over the line. The 70% rule's undercount is not only a Rabia issue.
- The 70% rule also cuts the other way: on field 20 (mostly unsown, west of Kirkuk) the top-crop map said 44% cereal but only an eastern strip grew; the rule's 24% was closer to the truth. So the rule trades undercounting real rain-fed cereal (fields 11, 16, 19) against overcounting on bare or marginal land. A random sample is what will show the net effect.
- **FTW coverage gap (random point 36.55100, 42.16461, south of Rabia towards Sinjar, map: wheat).** The satellite view shows very large rain-fed fields (around 1 km across), but FTW has no polygon over them: at zoom 14 only 16 slivers, the largest 2.4 ha, none under the point. Worth measuring how much of the Rabia–Sinjar plain FTW misses.

## Lessons so far
- Late-field test 2 (fields 198–211, 31 Oct 2026): same design and the same prediction (at least 6 of 10 Partly or No). Search area: northern May-window tiles from latitude 36.3; after the Nineveh plain gave no late fields, the search was limited to the Rabia belt west of 42.5°E. Result: of 10 scored fields, 7 wrong (5 Partly, 2 No), 3 Yes; one of the Yes is a spring-sown potato crop that the map correctly called not cereal. A new rule was needed: a crop whose green-up starts in March (February below 0.15 and March below 0.25) can't be told apart from a spring crop, so it isn't scored. Four fields fell under this rule (201, 202, 204, 206); WorldCereal called all four vegetables or tobacco, which may well be right. Combined with everything before: late northern winter cereal is wrong 27 of 36 times (75%) against 26 of 61 (43%) for earlier northern cereal (Fisher's exact test p ≈ 0.003). Late fields cluster west of Rabia near the Syrian border; on the Nineveh and Erbil plains almost every screened field had dried down by May.
- Late-field test (fields 188–197, 30 Oct 2026): prediction made before reading the map: at least 6 of 10 late northern fields (April ≥ 0.35, May ≥ 0.30, July ≤ 0.20, in tiles with the 31 May season end) would come out Partly or No. Result: 7 of 10 (5 Partly, 2 No). Across all checks, late northern cereal fields were wrong 20 of 27 times (74%) against 26 of 61 (43%) for earlier northern cereal fields (Fisher's exact test p ≈ 0.01). The 31 May season end is a real factor. But late fields are uncommon: about 12 of the 106 northern fields screened in this test were late crops, and two-thirds of the northern errors so far are not late fields, so the 70% rule and the vegetables/pulses confusion still matter more overall. Search points were drawn at random within the May-window tiles; the last batch was limited to the Rabia belt (lat ≥ 36.4, lon ≤ 42.9), where late fields are commonest. One screened field turned out to be trees (green all year) and was excluded; the screen then added July ≤ 0.20.
- Season window (from plan/grid_all.geojson): 206 of 218 tiles, about 96% of farmland, use WorldCereal's season of 1 Dec 2025 – 31 May 2026; only 12 tiles along the northern border and in the far east of Sulaymaniyah governorate run to June–August. Northern rain-fed cereal peaks in April and is often still green at the end of May. Among northern cereal fields checked so far, the share still at 0.30 or more in May is 4 of 39 for Yes, 8 of 28 for Partly and 5 of 11 for No: late fields are missed more often. This suggests, but doesn't prove, that cutting the season at 31 May hides the ripening and harvest the model expects.
- Fields 138–187 (random draw, 50 fields): 9 of the 10 irrigated cereal fields in the centre and south scored Yes (the tenth, a thin barley in Maysan, was Partly at 48% confident). In the north, rain-fed cereal split three ways: 13 Yes, 9 Partly, 5 No. The 5 misses (150, 157, 176, 178, 186) are again cereal-shaped crops read as vegetables, pulses, no crop or not cropland, now seen on the Sharazur plain and around Qaradagh as well as Nineveh and Rabia.
- Fields 108–137 (random draw, 30 fields): one more vegetables miss south of Rabia (137), making 5 such misses on the northern rain-fed plains. Not-scored cases are mostly weak growth (peak 0.2–0.35) where thin cereal and grass look alike from space, plus mixed tree cover and merged polygons; a field visit or high-resolution imagery is needed to settle those.
- Fields 78–107 (random draw, 30 fields): 4 misses, all rain-fed crops with a clear cereal curve on the Nineveh and Erbil plains or near Chamchamal (86, 90, 97, 102) that WorldCereal read as vegetables or not cropland. And for the first time irrigated cereal was cut by the 70% rule too (79 at 13%, 81 at 36%), so the problem isn't only rain-fed land.
- Fields 57–77 (random draw, 21 fields): all 5 cereal fields in the irrigated centre and south (58, 62, 66, 69, 72) scored Yes, while 5 of 7 northern rain-fed cereal fields scored Partly (confident 26–41%, top crop 54–100% cereal). The 70% rule is the main source of error in rain-fed land; field 76 shows it can cut even a strong crop (0.56 in April) to 41%.
- Fields with a weak, late green-up (peak around 0.25 in April) can be labelled as a crop (e.g. vegetables) by WorldCereal: cropland may be overstated in marginal land. Cereal is not affected.
- A field whose 2025-26 breakdown is a mix of many unlikely crops (tobacco, rapeseed, pulses on the Erbil plain) usually had little growth that season, not a different crop.
- Wheat stays green into April; barley usually fades about two weeks earlier. This is only a hint from the curve, not proof.
- Stepping through Copernicus Browser true-colour images from early May to early June is a quick, convincing way to see when a field ripened: green, then yellow, then straw. Ripening at the same time as the neighbours suggests the same crop.
- Iraq Farms Health's month-by-month playback (and now the Report's monthly images) shows different parts of one field side by side. It is the quickest way to spot a field that is really two plots sown at different times.
- In the rain-fed north-west, WorldCereal can split real cereal between "wheat (doubtful)", "vegetables" and "pulses". The 70% rule can undercount cereal there, the opposite of the hillside overcount seen near Erbil.
- Odd labels in straight-edged blocks (tobacco, potatoes) can mean a genuinely different crop with a different calendar, e.g. spring-sown chickpea. The crop name is unreliable, but "not winter cereal" may well be right. A rain-fed curve with an irrigated-crop label is a reason to look, not proof of an error.
- Irrigated cereal areas in the south and centre (Najaf, Babil, pivots north of Baghdad and Erbil) are where the map is most reliable, matching the 2021 comparison (Najaf agreement 0.82). The southern calendar is about a month earlier than the north: green from January, harvested late April–May.
- Low 2021 cereal on a field that is clearly cereal now is usually the 2021 drought (land unsown or failed that year), not a mapping error in either year.
- Even a weak, patchy cereal field (field 9: peak only 0.55, thin until March) still came out as confident wheat. The "doubtful" pixels sit on the field's edge, where 10 m pixels mix with the canal bank or the next plot.
- A large FTW polygon can merge several parcels sown at different times or left bare (field 12, 57 ha). The 2026 map follows the parcels inside it: its no-crop pixels sat exactly on the parcels that stayed bare. Judge such fields parcel by parcel from the month images, not from the field-average curve.
- Random draws started at field 10 (field 11 was picked by hand). Field 12 is the first random cropland point outside the Rabia belt.
- Bias in the sample so far: 6 of 9 fields are irrigated, where the map does well, and all were chosen by hand. For a fair accuracy picture, more rain-fed fields (Nineveh, Sinjar, Kirkuk uplands, Erbil plain) and, eventually, randomly drawn points are needed.

## What these checks can and can't prove
- Per field, the greenness curve plus month images is enough evidence for "winter cereal or not" when the curve is clear (green-up in winter, peak Mar–Apr, harvest by May–June). Record such fields as "cereal: sure".
- It is not enough for wheat versus barley: only a farmer, a field visit or a dated photo can settle that.
- It is not enough for an accuracy figure: hand-picked fields over-represent easy, clear-cut fields. A publishable accuracy needs points drawn at random within each map class, labelled the same way.

## Data-quality notes
- A month value of exactly 0.00 (e.g. July 2026 at field 6, scene 2026-07-31) is suspicious for bare soil, which normally reads 0.05–0.10. It may be a bad scene (water, shadow or no-data). Worth a look in the NDVI pipeline.
- A negative month value is a bad scene, not bare soil: field 23 read −0.10 for October 2026 (14 Oct scene, speckled month image). The app showed it as that month's reading. Worth rejecting field medians below about 0 as masked/cloudy and trying the next scene. It is now frequent: fields 23, 24 and 25 all had a bad late-October scene (−0.10 on 14 Oct; 0.01 and −0.16 on 29 Oct), and on field 25 the farmer-facing headline read "−0.16 · Poor" for an irrigated field. Fix priority: high.
- A month's value can come from a scene that covers only a sliver of the field (an image at the edge of the satellite's swath). Field 28's February (0.58) and March (0.62) images show only a thin strip of the field; the value is a median of that strip. Harmless on a uniform field, misleading on a mixed one. Worth requiring a scene to cover most of the field before using it.
- FTW can produce giant polygons that aren't fields (field 26: 2,412 ha east of Kut). The app opens them, re-frames to minimum zoom, and loads slowly. Worth refusing polygons above a size cap (e.g. 500 ha) with a short message.
- The field's monthly value is the **median** NDVI of the polygon. On a polygon that merges many parcels, the median falls on the bare majority: field 13 read 0.09 on 18 March while its month image showed roughly a third of it green. A farmer sees "Poor" while parts of the field are healthy. Worth a methodology note, and possibly a "mixed field" flag when the spread inside the polygon is wide.
- A field is only whole if all of it is on screen when tapped. Pieces of a field come from `queryRenderedFeatures`, which returns only what is drawn, so a field running off the edge of the view is stitched without its off-screen part. Field 16 read 23.5 ha in Claude's narrow browser pane and 27.5 ha on Rawaz's screen: the crop share (46% vs 51%), the field verdict ("doubtful cereal" vs wheat) and some months (July 2025: 0.12 vs 0.23) all changed, while the FTW ID stayed the same. Until fixed, check the field size matches between runs, and zoom out so the whole field is in view before reading.
- **Build -j side effect: zooming out reads simplified field outlines.** The FTW PMTiles hold full detail only at tile zoom 15. When -j re-frames a field below zoom 15, the outline comes from simplified zoom-13/14 tiles, so the same field can come back a different size: field 28 read 29.9 ha at zoom 14.6 and 48.05 ha at zoom 13.9 (verdict wheat vs "doubtful cereal"). Fields re-framed below 15 (16, 19, 20, 27, 28) carry this caveat. Zooming out also starts health colouring for every field in the wider view, which on Rawaz's phone set off a Planetary Computer request storm (network failures, the app's breaker paused requests, the field loaded no months). Fix for -k: collect the off-screen pieces by panning at zoom ≥15 instead of zooming out, and hold back health colouring while the tapped field loads. Field 16 re-read on -k (16 panned views at zoom 16): 27.51 ha, 51% confident wheat, ID FTW-V3QXMM again, matching Rawaz's full-field run, so it stands. Field 20 was read below zoom 15 and should be re-read on -k; fields 19 (z15.1) and 27 (z15.0–15.3) were read at full detail.
